# Gut–Brain Evidence Review for the Stick-Pack PDP

The existing Prebiotic + Probiotic card linked one laboratory survival study and one human microbiome study, but neither directly addressed cognition, stress, or mood. The replacement set prioritizes human randomized trials that connect prebiotic supplementation with both microbiome and brain-related outcomes.

| Source | Human intervention | Relevant finding | Customer-facing limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| PROMOTe randomized controlled trial | 72 adults aged 60 or older received 7.5 g/day of an inulin + FOS blend or maltodextrin for 12 weeks; all participants also received BCAAs and exercise guidance | The prebiotic group improved a composite cognition factor and made fewer paired-associates-learning errors; *Bifidobacterium* abundance also increased.[1] | The studied dose was higher than BrewNectar’s 2 g inulin dose, the intervention combined inulin with FOS, and the sample was older adults. |
| EFFICAD randomized controlled trial | 92 adults with mild-to-moderate anxiety/depression received 8 g/day oligofructose, 2’FL, their combination, or maltodextrin for four intervention weeks | Oligofructose increased several beneficial bacterial taxa and outperformed maltodextrin on several mood-state measures.[2] | This tested oligofructose rather than BrewNectar’s exact inulin/probiotic blend and used a higher dose. |
| Focused review of prebiotics and cognitive resilience | Expert review of human intervention evidence and research gaps | The review explains plausible microbiota–gut–brain pathways while concluding that human cognitive evidence remains limited and heterogeneous.[3] | This is a scientific review, not a product trial, and should be presented as research context rather than proof of a BrewNectar outcome. |

The customer-facing summaries must identify the studied ingredient, dose, duration, and population where space allows. They should not claim that BrewNectar’s 2 g inulin dose reproduces the findings from 7.5–8 g/day trials.

The Alpha-GPC search found no additional strong healthy-adult cognition trials beyond the three already linked. Adding disease-treatment studies would make the card look larger but less relevant to the product’s healthy-user positioning. Alpha-GPC should therefore retain its three transparent studies and move below higher-evidence ingredients in the mobile card order.
